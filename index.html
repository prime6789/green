01 / The big picture

A building is more than
concrete and glass.

Buildings use resources throughout their life — from construction to everyday operation. Environmental management helps reduce their impact without sacrificing comfort, safety or usefulness.

The simple definition
“A green building is designed, built and operated to use resources wisely and create a healthier environment for people.”

It considers the whole life of a building — not only how it looks on opening day.

What makes it green?

  • Uses less energy and water than a comparable conventional building.
  • Reduces pollution and construction waste.
  • Makes use of daylight, shade and natural ventilation where practical.
  • Chooses durable, suitable and lower-impact materials.
  • Tracks performance and improves it over time.
02 / Environmental management toolkit

Design with nature.
Manage with data.

Good green buildings start with simple design choices, then combine efficient systems with careful operation and regular measurement.

☀️
Passive design

Work with the climate

Orient the building thoughtfully. Use shade, trees, insulation and suitable windows to reduce heat gain and cooling needs.

⚡
Energy

Use less electricity

LED lights, efficient fans and cooling, occupancy sensors and smart controls reduce avoidable energy use.

☀
Renewables

Generate cleaner power

Rooftop solar can provide part of a building’s electricity, depending on roof space, sunlight and system design.

💧
Water management

Capture and reuse

Rainwater harvesting, low-flow fixtures, leak checks and treated greywater reuse can lower demand for fresh water.

♻️
Circular materials

Keep resources in use

Plan quantities carefully, choose durable materials and separate construction waste for suitable reuse or recycling.

📡
Smart monitoring

Spot waste early

Sensors and dashboards can track energy, water and indoor conditions, helping managers detect unusual use or faults.

The management cycle

A practical four-step approach

STEP 01

Plan

Study climate, site, resources and user needs before construction.

STEP 02

Implement

Install efficient systems and use suitable materials and methods.

STEP 03

Monitor

Record energy, water, waste and indoor comfort data.

STEP 04

Improve

Compare results, fix problems and set new targets.

04 / Indian context

Local challenges.
Practical Indian solutions.

India’s climates, water availability, construction practices and energy needs vary widely. Green design should respond to local conditions rather than follow a single formula.

Project example / Gurugram

ITC Green Centre

ITC Green Centre is a widely cited early green-building example in India. The project announcement described energy-efficient design, water conservation, treated wastewater reuse and lower-impact materials.

53%Reported energy savings*
40%Reported reduction in potable water use*

*Figures reported in ITC’s 2004 project announcement against its stated conventional-building comparison. They are project-specific, not guaranteed outcomes for other buildings.

What can be applied elsewhere?

Principles for Indian cities

  • Shade, trees and climate-sensitive design to manage heat.
  • Rainwater harvesting and safe reuse where suitable.
  • Efficient cooling and rooftop solar where feasible.
  • Local, durable materials and planned construction waste management.
  • Metering and maintenance to check performance after occupancy.
India’s tools and frameworks

Guidance for better buildings

GRIHA

Green rating system

India’s national green building rating system evaluates environmental performance across areas such as energy, water, materials and site planning.

IGBC

Green building ratings

The Indian Green Building Council offers rating systems for different building types, helping project teams plan and assess sustainability measures.

BEE / ECBC

Energy performance

The Bureau of Energy Efficiency’s Energy Conservation Building Code provides energy-performance requirements for covered commercial buildings, as adopted by relevant authorities.

05 / Final takeaway

Green buildings are not just a trend. They are a way to manage resources responsibly.

The strongest approach combines thoughtful design, efficient systems, water and waste management, renewable energy where practical, and regular performance checks. The aim is simple: reduce environmental impact while creating useful, healthy spaces for people.
